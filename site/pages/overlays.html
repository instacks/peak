How they're placed

Where the browser supports CSS anchor positioning, a menu sits below its button and flips above or to the other side when there's no room. Elsewhere, menus open centered over a lightly dimmed page, like a small dialog. Either way they open and close with a short fade and slide (none with reduced motion).

Menus

A <ul popover> (or <menu popover>) opened by a button is a menu, styled by Blades and anchored by Peak. Put the button and the list in the same <li>, or wrap them in an element with data-peak-anchor, so the menu anchors to its own button even when several menus share a page.

Dropdowns from details

Pico's <details class="dropdown"> keeps working as before: absolutely positioned, no flipping. Add data-peak-anchor to anchor and flip it like a popover menu. It's opt-in because an ancestor with a transform or filter (common in animated page builders) would become its containing block. For new markup prefer the popover menu above.

Popovers

Any other element with popover is a panel on the raised surface, centered by the browser. Add data-peak-anchor to the popover, or to a wrapper around the trigger and the popover, to place it under its trigger instead.

Centered

The browser's default place. Press Esc or click outside to close.

Placed under its button, flipping at the edges.

The popover is at most 32rem wide. --peak-popover-offset sets how far it slides while it fades in (0.25rem; negative slides up).

Tooltips

Pico's data-tooltip works as before. Where the browser supports anchor positioning, Peak places the bubble with it, so it flips at the viewport edges and isn't cut off by a scrolling container. data-placement: top (default), bottom, left, right.

Hint popovers

A native alternative: popover="hint" with role="tooltip", styled like a tooltip and anchored above its trigger. Opening it on hover and focus needs interestfor (Chromium only so far); elsewhere a popovertarget opens it on click.
