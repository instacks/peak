What a year of sketchbooks taught us about planning

We kept one sketchbook per project for twelve months. The plans were wrong; the margins were where the useful ideas lived.

NH

18 November 2026 · 9 min read

At the start of the year we made one rule for the studio: every project gets its own sketchbook, and nothing about the project is planned anywhere else. No boards, no documents, no tickets. Twelve months and nineteen sketchbooks later, here is what we found.

The rule #

A sketchbook is opened on the first day of a project and closed on the last. The first page is the plan: what we're making, for whom, by when. Every page after that is dated.

  • One book per project, never shared between two.
  • Pages are dated, nothing is torn out.
  • The plan on page one is never edited, only answered later in the book.

What the plans got wrong #

Almost everything about time. Of nineteen plans, three finished on the date on page one. The others weren't late because of the work we had planned; they were late because of work that only appeared once we started.

The plan was never the useful part. Writing it was.

Ravi, in the margin of book 7

Estimates #

We now write an estimate as a range and a reason: two to four weeks, because we haven't used this veneer before. The reason is what we check later.

The margins #

The ideas we kept came from the margins: a note beside a sketch, a question written sideways. We started marking them with a star, and once a week someone copies the starred notes into a plain text file. We search it with Ctrl + F, which is enough. 

2026-03-14  shelf   ★ the back panel can be the bracing
2026-04-02  lamp    ★ cord exits through the base, not the arm
2026-06-19  stool   ★ test the joint in cardboard first

Each line is a date, the project's short name and the note. The file is called margins.txt.

Try the star

You don't need a sketchbook for this. Any notes you take by hand or in a file can get a margin and a symbol.

Try it #

  1. Pick your next project, however small.
  2. Write the plan on page one, with a date.
  3. Star what surprises you, and read the stars once a week.
